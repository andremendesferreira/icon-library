How to use it

  1. Choose a set in the Select button. The page opens with Lucide, or with the set you chose last time, and each set stays cached while the page is open.
  2. Narrow it down with All categories (you can tick several) and the search field — press / to jump to it. It matches icon names and keywords, so “delete” also finds the trash can, and the best matches come first. Names and keywords are in English. A search looks in All sets at once, with one section per set; pick a set in the selector to search only in it, and clearing the search takes you back to the set you were in. When nothing matches in a single set, the page offers to clear the search or the categories and to try the same word in the other sets. In Bootstrap Icons, Outline and Filled show only one of the two versions most icons have (?style=outline or ?style=filled in the address).
  3. Set the style in the Size, Stroke and Color controls. They apply to everything you copy, download or send to the scratchpad, and the preview follows the stroke and color. Stroke only exists in Lucide and Feather; emojis keep their own colors. Decorative adds aria-hidden="true", so screen readers skip an icon that sits next to a text label.
  4. Click an icon to copy its SVG (or the emoji character). Right-click copies the alternative: the icon name for vector sets, or the emoji wrapped in an SVG.
  5. Use the buttons on each card:
    • + adds the icon to the scratchpad;
    • ⋯ opens downloads (SVG, PNG at the chosen size) and copies the icon as a React, Vue or Svelte component, a data URI or a CSS class that takes the text color;
    • the pencil (all vector sets) opens the icon editor.

The icon editor

The pencil on a card opens the icon on a grid, ready to change. The icon arrives with the size, stroke and color you chose.

  • Reshape: select a shape in the list or on the grid and drag its points. Curves show their control points, linked by dashed lines; moving a point of the curve brings its controls along. Click a point and use + Curved point to add one with handles in the middle of the next segment, Curve point to give a corner two handles, or Straighten point to make it a corner again.
  • Move: drag a shape, or use the arrow keys or buttons. Points snap to the grid (1, ½ or ¼ unit, or off).
  • Transform: rotate 90° or 45°, flip, and grow or shrink — the selected shape, or the whole icon when nothing is selected.
  • Add and remove: new lines, circles, rectangles and curves; hide a shape to see the icon without it, or delete it. The shape buttons also add triangles, pentagons, hexagons, octagons, diamonds, stars, half and quarter circles, arrows, hearts and speech bubbles. The padlock in the list locks a shape: it stays in the drawing and in the SVG, but a click can no longer select, move or delete it until you unlock it there.
  • Trace over an image: under Reference image, choose a picture from your computer to show under the grid, with opacity from 40% to 100%. It stays on your screen only: it never goes into the SVG, the .ile file or the server, and closing the editor removes it.
  • Combine icons: Icon on top searches Lucide, Feather and Bootstrap and lays another icon over the current one, resized to the same grid and keeping its own style — a filled Bootstrap star can sit inside an outline Lucide heart. Each icon gets a heading in the list that selects it whole, to move, rotate or resize it.
  • Select several shapes: drag on an empty area and everything the rectangle touches is selected. Hold Shift to add to the selection, starting the drag anywhere, or Shift+click to add or remove one shape. Ctrl+A selects all and Esc clears. Move, rotate, resize, recolor, reorder, copy or delete them together. Group (Ctrl+G) keeps them together; a click then selects the whole group and a double click picks one shape. An icon added on top works the same way, and Ungroup (Ctrl+Shift+G) releases the shapes.
  • Join, split and order: Join (Ctrl+J) turns two open lines into one, through their nearest ends. With one open line selected, it closes the shape; if you also select a point, it connects the nearest end to that point. Split turns the cursor into a target: click where the line should be cut, or press Esc to cancel. The four order buttons in Transform send a shape backward or forward, or straight to the back or front. The ? button, or the ? key, shows every command with its keys and mouse actions.
  • Zoom: the mouse wheel, the magnifier buttons above the drawing or the + and − keys zoom in around the pointer; on a phone, pinch with two fingers. Hold Space and drag (or drag with the middle button) to move the view, and click the percentage, or press 0, to see the whole drawing again.
  • Resize and rotate by hand: the selection gets a box with handles. Drag a corner to resize, keeping the proportions and stopping on the grid; drag the dot above the box to rotate in 15° steps. Hold Shift for free values. For exact numbers, type X, Y, W and H in the Selected panel.
  • Align, snap and duplicate: the Align buttons line up several shapes with each other — or center one shape, a group or the whole icon in the icon area — and distribute three or more evenly. While you drag, points and shapes snap to the other shapes and to the center of the icon, with pink guide lines; hold Alt to drag freely. Duplicate (Ctrl+D) makes a copy next to the selection, and Shift+arrow moves ten grid steps.
  • Draw with the pen: the pen button, next to the ready-made shapes, lets you draw your own line. Click to add points, or drag while adding one to make a curve. Click the first point to close the shape; Enter or Done finishes an open line, and Esc cancels.
  • Line ends, corners and a final check: Ends (round, flat or square) and Corners (round, sharp or beveled) change how the line finishes. Hold Original to see the icon as you opened it, compare the previews on a light and a dark background, and Optimize, above the code, removes what doesn’t change the drawing.
  • Nothing gets lost: after deleting, splitting, joining or optimizing, the message at the bottom has an Undo button. The edit in progress is also kept in your browser: if the page reloads or the tab closes, the editor offers to recover it the next time it opens; closing the editor yourself discards it. In the list, drag a shape to change its order inside its icon or group, and ✎ renames a group. With the drawing focused, Tab picks the next shape, and screen readers announce which one.
  • On a phone: the drawing stays in view, with the tools in tabs below it: Shapes, Edit, Transform, Elements and Export. A bar next to the drawing has Undo, Redo, Delete and Several, where each tap adds or removes a shape. You can also hold a shape for half a second to add or remove it. The first tap only selects a shape, so touching it doesn't move it by accident; drag it once it's selected.
  • Fill a shape: with a closed shape selected, Fill paints its inside in the color of its line, and the same button removes it. To fill an open line, close it with Join first.
  • Gradient: open Gradient under the selected shape and click a suggestion (sunset, ocean, purple…), or pick two colors and click Apply gradient. Choose linear (with a direction) or radial, and whether it goes on the fill (closed shapes) or the stroke, which also works on straight lines. One gradient covers all the selected shapes and follows them when you move or resize them. Remove gradient keeps the first color. The ⇆ button next to the two colors swaps their order. Icons with gradients can be published to Iconlibrary too.
  • Combine closed shapes: select two closed shapes (circles, rectangles, polygons, closed paths) and use the buttons in Combine: Unite merges them into one (it also takes three or more), Subtract cuts the top one out of the one below, Intersect keeps only the overlap and Exclude keeps everything but the overlap. The result is a single path in place of the bottom shape, with its color and stroke, and curves stay curves. Undo brings the shapes back.
  • See the order: Show layers, above the element list, paints each shape in its own color and marks which one is at the front and at the back. A group counts as one block, with one color. It only changes the view: the icon stays the same.
  • Colors: give each shape, or each icon, its own color and stroke width in the Selected panel. Text color goes back to inheriting the color of the surrounding text; shapes with their own color keep it wherever the icon is pasted.
  • Save and continue later: Save .ile downloads the project as a .ile file — a small text file that keeps what plain SVG loses: which icon each shape came from, hidden shapes and the name. Open it again with Open .ile, in the editor or next to the search on the main page, or drop the file on the editor. Opening a file while editing can be undone.
  • Code: the SVG code beside the grid follows every change, and you can also type in it. Previews show the result at 16, 24, 32 and 48 pixels.
  • Undo and Redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y), then copy, download or send the edited icon to the scratchpad. Lucide and Feather icons can also be opened in Lucide Studio, an independent online editor with more advanced tools.

The scratchpad

The Scratchpad tab on the right edge opens a drawer where you can collect icons while you browse. Each icon is added as an HTML comment with its name followed by its SVG, so the result is ready to paste into a project. You can also type freely. Copy all copies everything and Clear empties it. The text is kept in your browser, so it is still there next time. Above the text, the icons it holds appear as a grid: click one to open it in the editor, and Save to scratchpad puts the edited version back in the same place. Upload SVG adds SVG files from your computer (you can also drop them on the grid), cleaned the same way as code pasted into the editor.

For AI assistants

AI assistants can create an icon and give it to you as a link that opens it here, in the editor, ready to adjust. The icon travels inside the link itself, after the #, so it is never sent to our server. In the editor, Share link makes the same kind of link for your own project. Instructions for AIs: llms.txt.

Sharing a search

The address bar follows what you are looking at — for example ?set=lucide&q=arrow. Copy the address to share a search or bookmark it.

Theme, language and preferences

The page follows your system's light or dark theme; the button in the header switches it. The language is detected from your browser and can be changed in the selector next to it. Both choices, the size, stroke and color settings and the scratchpad text are kept in your browser, so they are still there next time.

Buy me a coffee

Icon Library is free. If it helps you, the Buy me a coffee button (paid securely through Stripe) helps pay for the server, the domain and the tools that keep it running. As a thank-you, for 30 days from the payment you can publish and edit your own icons on Iconlibrary, and you won't see ads when the site shows them.

After paying you get a supporter code. To use these benefits on another device too — up to 3 —, open the same button there and enter the code. The button only appears when payments are available.

Supporters can also rate Icon Library from 1 to 5 stars, with a comment, on the About page. Sign in with GitHub, Google or Microsoft for a “verified” badge, or just type a name. Each review is checked before it appears.

Publishing to Iconlibrary

Iconlibrary is the community icon set: icons drawn in the editor by supporters, reviewed and shared with everyone. It appears in the set list like the others.

  1. Open an icon in the editor and draw or adjust it.
  2. Click Publish to Iconlibrary, give it a name, choose up to 3 categories and, if you like, a credit (your name or site).
  3. Your supporter code is filled in automatically on the device where you bought the coffee; on another device, type it.
  4. Confirm that you created the icon (or have the right to share it) and release it under the MIT license, then send it. It appears in Iconlibrary after review.

Only basic shapes can be published — lines, circles, ellipses, rectangles, polylines, polygons and paths — with solid colors or the editor's gradients. Brand logos and copies of other icons are not accepted.
